# Vectors

A **scalar** is described by one number. Mass, temperature, and time are scalars. A **vector** needs both a size and a direction. Displacement, velocity, acceleration, and force are vectors.

You have actually been working with vectors since grade school. When you first learned addition, a bunny hopped along a number line: start at $0$, hop $+3$, then hop $+2$, and land on $5$. Each hop has a **size** (how far) and a **direction** (right for positive, left for negative). That is already a vector — it just lives on a one-dimensional line.

<InteractiveAnchor id="bunny-number-line" />

<BunnyNumberLine client:visible />

Give the bunny a second way to hop — up and down as well as left and right — and a one-dimensional hop becomes a two-dimensional **vector**. The rest of this page is really the bunny learning to hop anywhere on the plane.

We draw a vector as an arrow. The tail marks where the arrow starts, the tip marks where it ends, and the arrow's length represents the vector's magnitude. Moving the whole arrow without turning or stretching it does not change the vector.

## Components

In two dimensions, a vector can be described by its horizontal and vertical pieces:

$$
\vec{a} = \langle a_x, a_y \rangle.
$$

The same vector can also be written with unit vectors:

$$
\vec{a} = a_x\hat{i} + a_y\hat{j}.
$$

Here, $\hat{i}$ points in the positive $x$ direction and $\hat{j}$ points in the positive $y$ direction. The components tell you how far the vector goes sideways and how far it goes vertically.

The magnitude of a vector is the length of the arrow:

$$
\lVert \vec{a} \rVert = \sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2}.
$$

The direction angle is usually measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis. For free-body diagrams, the most important habit is to keep the components and the drawn arrow consistent.

## Addition

To add vectors graphically, place the tail of the second vector at the tip of the first. The sum points from the original tail to the final tip. This is called **head-to-tail addition**.

Symbolically, add matching components:

$$
\vec{a} + \vec{b}
= \langle a_x, a_y \rangle + \langle b_x, b_y \rangle
= \langle a_x + b_x, a_y + b_y \rangle.
$$

  <WorkedExampleCard
    title="Reading a Vector"
    accent="blue"
  >
    #### Problem

    A vector has components $\vec{a} = \langle 3, 4 \rangle$. Find its magnitude.

    #### Use

    $$
    \lVert \vec{a} \rVert = \sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2}.
    $$

    #### Solve

    $$
    \lVert \vec{a} \rVert = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}
    = \sqrt{25}
    = 5.
    $$

    #### Check

    A vector that moves $3$ units sideways and $4$ units vertically forms a $3$-$4$-$5$ right triangle, so a magnitude of $5$ is reasonable.
  </WorkedExampleCard>

  <WorkedExampleCard
    title="Adding Two Hops"
    accent="blue"
  >
    #### Problem

    A bunny hops $\vec{a} = \langle 2, 5 \rangle$, then hops $\vec{b} = \langle -1, 3 \rangle$. Where does it land relative to the start?

    #### Add

    $$
    \vec{a} + \vec{b}
    = \langle 2 + (-1), 5 + 3 \rangle
    = \langle 1, 8 \rangle.
    $$

    #### Check

    Add matching components: the $x$ values partly cancel to $1$, and the $y$ values build to $8$, so the bunny ends up $\langle 1, 8 \rangle$ from where it started.
  </WorkedExampleCard>
