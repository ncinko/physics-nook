# 1D Kinematics

Kinematics describes **how motion changes** without asking what caused the motion. In one dimension, the whole story can be told with three time-dependent quantities: position, velocity, and acceleration.

- **Position** tells us where an object is along a line.
- **Velocity** tells us how quickly position changes, including direction.
- **Acceleration** tells us how quickly velocity changes, including direction.

The sign matters. If rightward motion is positive, then a negative velocity means motion to the left. A negative acceleration means the velocity is becoming more negative, not automatically that the object is slowing down.

## Velocity

Average velocity compares two positions at two times:

$$
\overline{v} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}
= \frac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1}.
$$

Instantaneous velocity is the velocity at one moment:

$$
v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt}.
$$

Graphically, average velocity is the slope of a secant line on a position-time graph. Instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent line at a single point.

<DerivationPanel title="Is velocity the same as speed?">
  Almost, but not quite. Speed only says how fast something moves. Velocity says how fast and in which signed direction. In one dimension, the speed is the absolute value of the velocity:

  $$
  \text{speed} = |v|.
  $$

  An object with $v = -4\,\text{m/s}$ and an object with $v = +4\,\text{m/s}$ have the same speed, but they are moving in opposite directions.
</DerivationPanel>

## Acceleration

Average acceleration compares two velocities at two times:

$$
\overline{a} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
= \frac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}.
$$

Instantaneous acceleration is the acceleration at one moment:

$$
a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt}.
$$

The slope idea repeats: acceleration is the slope of a velocity-time graph in the same way velocity is the slope of a position-time graph.

<DerivationPanel title="How can negative acceleration speed something up?">
  Negative acceleration means the velocity is changing in the negative direction. If an object is already moving left with $v < 0$, then negative acceleration makes the velocity more negative and the object speeds up. If an object is moving right with $v > 0$, the same negative acceleration makes the object slow down until the velocity reaches zero.
</DerivationPanel>
