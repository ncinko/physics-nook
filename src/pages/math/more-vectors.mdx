# More with Vectors

On the [first vectors page](/math/vectors) a bunny hopped: every hop had a magnitude and a direction, and chaining hops tip-to-tail added the vectors. Here we keep working with that same hop, but stretch it, flip it, break it into unit-sized steps, and finally attach the physical units that turn an abstract arrow into a displacement, a velocity, or a force.

## Scaling and Negation

Multiplying a vector by an ordinary number — a **scalar** — stretches or shrinks it without changing the line it points along. Each component is multiplied by the scalar:

$$
c\,\vec{a} = c\,\langle a_x, a_y \rangle = \langle c\,a_x,\; c\,a_y \rangle.
$$

The magnitude scales by the absolute value of the scalar,

$$
\lVert c\,\vec{a} \rVert = |c|\,\lVert \vec{a} \rVert,
$$

so $2\vec{a}$ is twice as long and $\tfrac{1}{2}\vec{a}$ is half as long, both pointing the same way as $\vec{a}$.

A **negative** scalar does something extra: it reverses the direction. The special case $c = -1$ is **negation**, $-\vec{a} = (-1)\vec{a}$, which is the same-length hop pointing the opposite way. This is exactly what lets us subtract: $\vec{a} - \vec{b} = \vec{a} + (-\vec{b})$.

## Unit Vectors

A **unit vector** is any vector with magnitude $1$. Two of them are special enough to get their own names. Let the bunny's single hop one space to the right be

$$
\hat{\imath} = \langle 1, 0 \rangle,
$$

and its single hop one space up be

$$
\hat{\jmath} = \langle 0, 1 \rangle.
$$

(The "hat" is read "i-hat" and "j-hat," and always marks a unit vector.) Using the scaling rule from the previous section, any vector is just a scaled right-hop plus a scaled up-hop:

$$
\vec{a} = \langle a_x, a_y \rangle = a_x\,\hat{\imath} + a_y\,\hat{\jmath}.
$$

So $\langle 3, 2 \rangle$ is "three hops right and two hops up," or $3\hat{\imath} + 2\hat{\jmath}$. Every press of an arrow key below adds one unit vector to the bunny's running **displacement** from where its current carrot appeared. Each hop is drawn as a blue vector; reach the carrot and their green sum is the total displacement — the same head-to-tail addition from the first page, now built one unit vector at a time.

## Vectors Carry Units

So far the bunny's hops have been pure numbers on a grid. In physics a vector almost always carries a **unit** that tells you what it measures. The bunny's hop is a **displacement**, so its components have units of length — say meters — and a hop of $\langle 3, 2 \rangle\ \text{m}$ means three meters east and two meters north. Displacement is the most intuitive vector precisely because you can pace it out.

The power of the vector formalism is that the *algebra never changes*. Adding components, scaling, and decomposing into $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$ work identically no matter what the arrow represents. The units simply ride along inside the magnitude:

<div className="my-6 flex justify-center [&_table]:!my-0 [&_table]:!w-auto [&_th]:!px-6 [&_td]:!px-6">

| Quantity | Symbol | Example units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Displacement | $\vec{d}$ | $\text{m}$ |
| Velocity | $\vec{v}$ | $\text{m/s}$ |
| Acceleration | $\vec{a}$ | $\text{m/s}^2$ |
| Force | $\vec{F}$ | $\text{N} = \text{kg·m/s}^2$ |
| Electric field | $\vec{E}$ | $\text{N/C}$ |

</div>

Scaling even respects the units. Multiplying a velocity $\vec{v}$ (in $\text{m/s}$) by a time $t$ (a scalar in $\text{s}$) gives a displacement in meters, $\vec{d} = \vec{v}\,t$ — the seconds cancel just as they would in ordinary arithmetic, while the *direction* of the velocity is preserved. Every tool you built on the first page now applies to forces on a free-body diagram, to electric fields around a charge, and to any other quantity with both a magnitude and a direction.

    accent="blue"
  >
    #### Problem

    A bunny's hop is $\vec{a} = \langle 2, 3 \rangle$. Find $2\vec{a}$ and $-\vec{a}$.

    #### Use

    $$
    c\,\langle a_x, a_y \rangle = \langle c\,a_x,\; c\,a_y \rangle.
    $$

    #### Solve

    $$
    2\vec{a} = \langle 2\cdot 2,\; 2\cdot 3 \rangle = \langle 4, 6 \rangle,
    \qquad
    -\vec{a} = \langle -2, -3 \rangle.
    $$

    #### Check

    $2\vec{a}$ points the same way as $\vec{a}$ but is twice as long, while $-\vec{a}$ has the same length and points the opposite way — exactly what scaling by $2$ and by $-1$ should do.
  </WorkedExampleCard>

  <WorkedExampleCard
    title="Unit Vectors and Units"
    accent="blue"
  >
    #### Problem

    A velocity is $\vec{v} = \langle -4, 3 \rangle\ \text{m/s}$. Write it with unit vectors and find its speed (the magnitude).

    #### Write and solve

    $$
    \vec{v} = -4\,\hat{\imath} + 3\,\hat{\jmath}\ \text{m/s},
    \qquad
    \lVert \vec{v} \rVert = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\ \text{m/s}.
    $$

    #### Check

    The components form a $3$-$4$-$5$ right triangle, so a speed of $5\ \text{m/s}$ is reasonable, and the unit $\text{m/s}$ carries straight through to the magnitude.
  </WorkedExampleCard>
