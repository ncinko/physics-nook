# Information and Entropy

Entropy began as a thermodynamic idea, but it keeps reappearing anywhere we reason about hidden possibilities. A macrostate leaves out microscopic details. Information tells us how many possibilities remain.

That connection is not just poetic. It becomes quantitative when we compare Boltzmann entropy, Shannon entropy, and the physical cost of erasing information.

## Missing Microstate Information

Boltzmann entropy,

$$
S = k_B \ln \Omega,
$$

grows when more microstates are compatible with the same macrostate. If all $\Omega$ microstates are equally plausible, identifying the actual microstate would require more information when $\Omega$ is larger.

In bits, the amount of information needed to distinguish $\Omega$ equally likely possibilities is

$$
I = \log_2 \Omega.
$$

The logarithm base changes the unit. Natural logarithms pair naturally with thermodynamic entropy; base-$2$ logarithms measure information in bits.

## Shannon Entropy

Shannon entropy measures uncertainty in a probability distribution:

$$
H = -\sum_i p_i \log_2 p_i.
$$

If a fair bit has $p_0 = p_1 = 1/2$, then

$$
H = -\frac{1}{2}\log_2\frac{1}{2}
    -\frac{1}{2}\log_2\frac{1}{2}
  = 1\,\text{bit}.
$$

If the bit is already known, for example $p_0 = 1$ and $p_1 = 0$, its Shannon entropy is $0$ bits. There is no missing logical information about which state it is in.

## Erasure Has a Thermal Cost

Erasing a bit means resetting it to a standard state, such as $0$, regardless of whether it started as $0$ or $1$. That operation compresses two possible logical states into one. If done reversibly as well as physics allows, the minimum energy dissipated to a thermal environment at temperature $T$ is Landauer's bound:

$$
E_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2.
$$

For $N$ erased bits,

$$
E_{\min} = N k_B T \ln 2.
$$

Real devices usually dissipate much more than this limit, but the bound is important because it says information processing is not floating above thermodynamics. Logical operations can have physical consequences.

## Where This Leads

The entropy-information bridge points far beyond introductory thermodynamics:

- **Black holes:** black-hole entropy suggests that horizon area counts hidden microscopic possibilities.
- **Quantum information:** entanglement entropy measures how much information about a subsystem is inaccessible when viewed alone.
- **Modern statistical physics:** entropy remains central because it links microscopic dynamics, macroscopic irreversibility, and what observers can know.

Entropy survives across so many areas because it is not only about heat. It is about the structure of possibilities.

  <WorkedExampleCard
    title="Minimum Energy to Erase One Bit"
    accent="blue"
  >
    #### Problem

    Find the Landauer minimum energy to erase one bit at room temperature, $T = 300\,\text{K}$.

    #### Given

    - $N = 1$
    - $T = 300\,\text{K}$
    - $k_B = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\,\text{J/K}$

    #### Use

    $$
    E_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2.
    $$

    #### Solve

    $$
    E_{\min}
    =
    (1.38 \times 10^{-23})(300)(0.693)
    \approx 2.87 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J}.
    $$

    #### Check

    The value is tiny for one bit, which is why Landauer's bound is usually discussed as a lower limit rather than the dominant energy cost in ordinary computers.
  </WorkedExampleCard>

  <WorkedExampleCard
    title="Compare Erasure Cost for Many Bits"
    accent="red"
  >
    #### Problem

    Estimate the minimum energy to erase $10^9$ bits at $300\,\text{K}$. Use the one-bit result $E_1 \approx 2.87 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J}$.

    #### Given

    - $N = 10^9$
    - $E_1 \approx 2.87 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J}$

    #### Use

    $$
    E_N = N E_1.
    $$

    #### Solve

    $$
    E_N
    =
    (10^9)(2.87 \times 10^{-21})
    =
    2.87 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{J}.
    $$

    #### Check

    The cost scales linearly with the number of erased bits. A billion bits is a lot of information, but the thermodynamic lower bound is still very small on everyday energy scales.
  </WorkedExampleCard>
