# Motion Match

Reading a motion graph and *being* a motion graph are different skills. This one asks for the second: three target curves appear, and you match each by walking in front of an ultrasonic motion detector while it records where you are twenty times a second.

Two of the targets are position against time, and the third is velocity against time — which is the one that catches people out, because a flat line no longer means standing still.

## What you need

- A **Vernier LabQuest Mini** (or LabQuest 2 or 3) connected by USB.
- A **Motion Detector** in the **DIG 1** port, with its sensitivity switch on the walking-figure setting.
- **Chrome or Edge.** Reading a USB instrument from a web page uses WebHID, which Firefox and Safari do not implement. There is a practice mode that works in any browser.
- About **two metres** of clear floor in front of the detector, and a clear line of sight — the sonar will happily range off a chair instead of you.

Put the detector at roughly waist height, pointed down the open space. Every target stays between 0.6 m and 2.3 m away, so a perfect match is genuinely walkable rather than a sprint.

## Reading your own trace

The green band is full credit — six centimetres either side of the target on a position graph, eight centimetres per second on a velocity graph. That is wide enough to absorb ordinary walking sway and narrow enough that you have to mean it.

Where a run usually goes wrong:

1. **Starting late.** The clock begins the instant the countdown ends, so the first flat section is part of the graph, not a grace period.
2. **Turning too gently.** A corner on a position graph is a real change of direction. Rounding it costs marks on both sides of the turn.
3. **On the velocity graph, drifting.** Holding a steady speed feels like doing nothing, which is exactly why it is hard. Watch the trace, not your feet.
4. **Losing the echo.** Gaps in your line mean the detector did not hear you. Arms at your sides, and nothing between you and the sensor.
