A charged particle sitting still in a magnetic field feels nothing.  If it's moving, the field pushes it *sideways*.  This magnetic force bends electron beams in old cathode-ray tubes, steers ions around the ring of a cyclotron, and guides charged particles from the Sun into spirals along Earth’s field lines. 

A particle with charge $q$ moving with velocity $\vec{\mathbf v}$ through a magnetic field $\vec{\mathbf B}$ feels the **magnetic force**

$$
\vec{\mathbf F} = q\,\vec{\mathbf v}\times\vec{\mathbf B}.
$$

This equation uses the cross product, a mathematical operation between two vectors that outputs a third:

- **Magnitude.** $F = |q|\,v\,B\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec{\mathbf v}$ and $\vec{\mathbf B}$. The force is greatest when the motion is perpendicular to the field and zero when it is parallel or antiparallel to it.
- **Direction.** Use the right-hand rule: point your index finger along $\vec{\mathbf v}$ and your middle finger along $\vec{\mathbf B}$. Your thumb points along $\vec{\mathbf F}$ for a *positive* charge. A negative charge is pushed the opposite way.

## Circular Motion

Suppose the field is uniform and the particle moves perpendicular to it. The force has constant magnitude and is always perpendicular to the velocity, which is precisely the conditions needed for uniform circular motion. <DerivationPanel title="Why does the speed stay constant?">

Work is the part of a force along the displacement. Since $\vec{\mathbf F}\perp\vec{\mathbf v}$ at every instant,

$$
\vec{\mathbf F}\cdot\vec{\mathbf v} = 0,
$$

so the magnetic force does no work and the particle’s kinetic energy cannot change. Only the direction of $\vec{\mathbf v}$ changes, never its magnitude.

</DerivationPanel>

<DerivationPanel title="Why is the path a circle, and how big is it?">

For a particle moving perpendicular to a uniform field, $\sin\theta=1$ and the force has the constant magnitude $|q|vB$, always perpendicular to the velocity. This is the condition for uniform circular motion, so the magnetic force is *centripetal*:

$$
|q|vB = \frac{mv^2}{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r = \frac{mv}{|q|B}.
$$

The radius is proportional to the particle’s momentum $mv$, which is how detectors measure momentum from the curvature of a track. The time for one "lap" is the circumference over the speed,

$$
T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|B}.
$$

Every particle of the same charge:mass ratio $q/m$ has the same orbital frequency in a uniform magnetic field.  This is an important consequence for the operation of cyclotrons.

</DerivationPanel>

<ProblemSolvingSection title="Problem Solving">
  <WorkedExampleCard title="A Proton in a Strong Field" accent="blue">
    #### Problem

    A proton enters a $0.80\,\text{T}$ field at $3.0\times10^{5}\,\text{m/s}$, perpendicular to the field. What is the radius of its path?

    #### Use

    $$
    r = \frac{mv}{|q|B}.
    $$

    #### Solve

    With $m_p = 1.67\times10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$ and $q = 1.60\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}$,

    $$
    r = \frac{(1.67\times10^{-27})(3.0\times10^{5})}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.80)} \approx 3.9\times10^{-3}\,\text{m} = 3.9\,\text{mm}.
    $$

    #### Check

    A field this strong bends even a fast proton into a small circle. The speed is only about $0.1\%$ of the speed of light, so a radius of a few millimeters is reasonable.
  </WorkedExampleCard>

  <WorkedExampleCard title="Orbit Time of an Electron" accent="red">
    #### Problem

    An electron moves perpendicular to a $0.050\,\text{T}$ field. How long does one lap take, and does the answer depend on its speed?

    #### Use

    $$
    T = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|B}.
    $$

    #### Solve

    With $m_e = 9.11\times10^{-31}\,\text{kg}$ and $|q| = 1.60\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}$,

    $$
    T = \frac{2\pi(9.11\times10^{-31})}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.050)} \approx 7.2\times10^{-10}\,\text{s} = 0.72\,\text{ns}.
    $$

    #### Check

    The speed does not appear in $T$, so the answer holds for any speed. Faster electrons trace bigger circles in the same time. This matches what the simulation’s period readout does when you change the launch speed.
  </WorkedExampleCard>
</ProblemSolvingSection>
