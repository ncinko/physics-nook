# Rotational Dynamics

Rotational dynamics studies objects that spin and the influences that change their spin. Every idea has a familiar linear analog: force becomes **torque**, mass becomes **moment of inertia**, and Newton's second law $\vec F = m\vec a$ becomes

$$
\tau = I\alpha.
$$

If forces and free-body diagrams feel rusty, the [Forces](/forces) page is a good warm-up — torque is built from forces and lever arms.

The key quantities:

- **Torque** $\tau = rF\sin\theta$ — the rotational equivalent of force, from a force $F$ applied at a lever arm $r$.
- **Moment of inertia** $I$ — resistance to changes in spin. For a point mass $I = mr^2$; for a solid disk about its center $I = \tfrac12 mr^2$.
- **Angular kinematics** — angular displacement $\theta$, angular velocity $\omega = \dfrac{d\theta}{dt}$, and angular acceleration $\alpha = \dfrac{d\omega}{dt}$.
- **Angular momentum** $L = I\omega$ — conserved when no external torque acts.
- **Rotational kinetic energy** $K_{\text{rot}} = \tfrac12 I\omega^2$.

## A Disk and a Torque

For a solid disk spinning about its center, $I = \tfrac12 mr^2$. Apply a net torque and it gains angular acceleration through $\tau = I\alpha$. Notice how a heavier or larger disk resists the same torque more.

## Lever Arm and Torque

A force produces more torque the farther from the pivot it acts, and the more perpendicular it is to the lever arm:

$$
\tau = rF\sin\theta,
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the force and the lever arm. A force pointing straight along the arm ($\theta = 0$) makes no torque at all.

## Physical Pendulum

A rigid body swinging about a pivot is a **physical pendulum**. Its angular frequency depends on the moment of inertia about the pivot and the distance $d$ from the pivot to the center of mass:

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{mgd}{I}}.
$$

Switching the disk between rigidly fixed and free to spin changes $I$ about the pivot, and so changes the period.

<ProblemSolvingSection title="Practice">
  <WorkedExampleCard title="Angular Acceleration of a Disk" accent="blue">
    #### Problem

    A solid disk of mass $4\,\text{kg}$ and radius $0.5\,\text{m}$ feels a net torque of $2\,\text{N·m}$. Find its angular acceleration.

    #### Solve

    $$
    I = \tfrac12 m r^2 = \tfrac12 (4)(0.5)^2 = 0.5\,\text{kg·m}^2,
    $$

    $$
    \alpha = \frac{\tau}{I} = \frac{2}{0.5} = 4\,\text{rad/s}^2.
    $$
  </WorkedExampleCard>

  <WorkedExampleCard title="Torque on a Wheel" accent="blue">
    #### Problem

    A force of $10\,\text{N}$ is applied tangentially to the rim of a wheel of radius $0.3\,\text{m}$. What is the torque?

    #### Solve

    Tangential means $\theta = 90°$, so

    $$
    \tau = rF\sin 90° = (0.3)(10) = 3\,\text{N·m}.
    $$
  </WorkedExampleCard>

  <WorkedExampleCard title="Angular Velocity from Angular Momentum" accent="blue">
    #### Problem

    A rotating object has angular momentum $8\,\text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}$ and moment of inertia $2\,\text{kg·m}^2$. Find its angular velocity.

    #### Solve

    From $L = I\omega$,

    $$
    \omega = \frac{L}{I} = \frac{8}{2} = 4\,\text{rad/s}.
    $$
  </WorkedExampleCard>

  <WorkedExampleCard title="Rotational Kinetic Energy" accent="red">
    #### Problem

    A disk with moment of inertia $0.5\,\text{kg·m}^2$ rotates at $4\,\text{rad/s}$. Find its rotational kinetic energy.

    #### Solve

    $$
    K_{\text{rot}} = \tfrac12 I\omega^2 = \tfrac12 (0.5)(4)^2 = 4\,\text{J}.
    $$
  </WorkedExampleCard>
</ProblemSolvingSection>
